## Driving and previewing

Choose a world seed, touring or GP style, gentle or rolling land, corner rhythm and
lap length. Touring supports **2.0–4.5 km**, GP **4.0–7.5 km**. Length targets are
horizontal alignment distance; the displayed driven lap includes elevation.
Mixed, flowing and technical rhythms alter the section grammar. These are game
design presets, not replicas or homologated circuit designs.

**Route A/B/C** changes the layout stream while keeping the natural terrain fixed.
Preview an alternative, then choose **Drive circuit**. The three most recent
previews are cached so comparisons do not repeatedly rebuild the same geometry.
Changing design inputs invalidates the displayed preview and disables driving until
the new result is ready. Overlay and diagnostic choices leave the result valid.
Generation runs in a worker with progress and cancellation. Closing the workshop
cancels an unfinished job; stale results cannot replace a newer preview.

Map overlays show actual sections, estimated speeds, a reference line, centreline
cut/fill, graded corridor width, facility/escape reservations and the site survey.
The elevation plot compares natural ground with the designed road. Inspect speed,
grade, banking, width, signed curvature and cut/fill along the lap. Speed models
cover touring/road, GT/hypercar and prototype/open-wheel envelopes. These use a
bounded minimum-curvature line and grade/bank/traction/acceleration/braking/vertical
load estimates; they are design aids, not predicted racing lap times or AI drivers.

The validation report and expandable design evidence expose rejected candidates,
proposal engine, search count, closing-section share, curve-fit deviation, corner
rhythm, earthworks and retained relief. Hull occupancy measures folding, not quality.
Long straights and relatively simple flowing venues remain valid intentional designs.

**Export recipe** opens inspectable JSON containing the recipe, fingerprint, final
definition and design report. **Download JSON** saves it as a file; **Copy JSON**
also selects the text for ordinary keyboard copying when a browser's clipboard
integration is unavailable or inconsistent.
This preserves the accepted geometry in addition to the procedural inputs. Recipes
carry version 2 and independent seed/layout choices. The last loaded recipe is
remembered; startup continues to open the authored M3 circuit. Loading/resetting a
circuit, changing reference cars or rebuilding a chassis preserves the active venue.
Vehicle setup persistence is independent of circuit recipes.

For earlier circuits, choose **Advanced design settings → V1 · legacy recipes**,
then restore the original seed, family, terrain and length. Its original limits and
generator are retained. Old v1 browser storage is preserved separately from v2.
The separate `circuit-lab.html` remains the historical research experiment; its
uncertified previews are not the production v2 driving generator.

## How the route is designed

1. **Survey the site first.** Seeded broad terrain supplies heights and slopes.
   A coarse feature graph identifies buildable patches, high/low regions and links
   weighted by distance and climbing cost. Connected itineraries from candidate
   facility patches guide the route's geographic targets and initial headings.
2. **Propose different spatial arrangements.** The sequence engine grows signed
   straights, sweepers, tightening pairs, esses, hairpins and braking chicanes.
   The anchor engine reserves several free straight entry/exit poses first, then
   fits signed biarc complexes between them. Neither requires angular order around
   a centre or a fixed library of whole-track outlines. Rhythm changes the mix.
3. **Distribute closure.** A biarc supplies an initial bounded connector. A damped
   solve shortens that connector and adjusts up to six upstream lengths/radii,
   within 22% bounds, while keeping signed sweeps and net turn. Oversized closure,
   crossings, inadequate corridor space, small radii or a lost grid are rejected.
4. **Fit a smooth alignment.** Uniform-distance samples feed a periodic C2 spline.
   This uses the revised plan's constrained spline backend. Actual radii, geometric
   grid length, deviation from the analytical proposal, crossings and corridor
   clearance are checked after fitting. Straight interiors remain collinear; curve
   labels are not accepted as evidence of actual geometry.
   The final curvature must also match the brief: technical designs need several
   meaningful alternating corner groups. A safe two-loop outline cannot qualify
   merely because its recipe says “technical”. Tiny kinks are excluded from this
   count. Flowing presets deliberately allow simpler layouts.
5. **Compare site fits.** Candidate position/headings are scored against facility
   grading, regional itinerary, terrain variation and earthwork proxies. A Pareto
   frontier retains alternatives across site fit, earthworks and motif variety.
   Both proposal engines can reach the full fitting stage. Detailed fitting failures
   move on to another candidate rather than forcing the same outline to pass.
6. **Fit horizontal and vertical geometry together.** A periodic elevation solve
   regularizes local terrain samples while projecting grade, vertical curvature
   and locked grid constraints. Locked grid points also constrain their neighbours.
   Excessive earthwork can trigger a bounded local bend edit followed by a new road
   fit and complete geometry validation. Straights/facilities are protected. Routes
   exceeding corridor-wide cut/fill or relief-retention budgets are rejected.
7. **Fit the cross-section.** Grid/braking roles drive width, with smooth tapers.
   Curved runs receive modest beneficial bank with reserved transitions; drainage
   crown stays separate. Tyre-path acceleration checks repair local bank regions.
   This does not reduce banking or flatten relief globally to hide an invalid fit.
8. **Compile the accepted surface.** Exact road/kerb/runoff triangles and constrained
   terrain tiles supply both rendering and collision. Float32 winding is checked
   before a preview can be loaded. A compilation failure rejects that candidate and
   continues the bounded search. No invalid mesh is returned as a successful seed.

The source project's mathematical foundations are attributed in
`design/primitives.ts`. Production route construction lives in `LayoutProposal.ts`,
site planning in `SiteGraph.ts`, profile fitting in `RoadProfiles.ts`, and line/speed
estimates in `ReferenceLines.ts`. `generateDesignedCircuit.ts` coordinates them.
`generateLegacyCircuit.ts` preserves v1, and `generateCircuit.ts` dispatches by recipe
version. Randomness never uses wall-clock time or a shared global stream.

## Terrain without radial restrictions

The compiler's v2 road ribbon ends at explicit graded corridor boundaries. Both
boundaries and a subdivided outer site rectangle constrain a world-space terrain
grid. Parity classification removes the road footprint and retains the outside land
and every concave infield bay. Every corridor vertex uses the same Float32 position
as its neighbouring road vertex. Terrain does not continue underneath the road.

`cdt2d` 1.0.0 supplies the constrained domain. A conservative local angle-refinement
pass improves only retained terrain triangles; boundary edges cannot flip, and
uncertain/cocircular decisions keep their diagonal. This avoids expensive exact
in-circle decisions in unused road-hole faces. Small deterministic offsets prevent
regular-grid degeneracies. Neither operation modifies the road boundary.

Nearby road legs impose simultaneous grading slope intervals. Incompatible heights
reject the candidate instead of producing a hidden ridge or intersecting fill.
Terrain tiles reuse boundary coordinates and are batched spatially. Trees sample
actual compiled terrain vertices and avoid the road, runoff and paddock. The
authored folded fixture checks hills, deep infield bays and opposing legs independently
of the procedural generator. Its only open mesh edges are the outer site perimeter.

The render and physics paths share typed vertex/index/material buffers. The existing
per-material collider mapping, adaptive road sampling, nearest-road spatial query,
vehicle interpolation, moving shadows and circuit-swap rollback remain in use.

## Bounds and qualification

| Property               | V2 rule                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Native proposal budget | Up to 6,400 proposals; up to eight feasible arrangements enter site fitting                                           |
| Closing connector      | At most 23% of the raw lap; upstream solve stays within 22% parameter edits                                           |
| Curve fitting          | At most 8 m analytical-proposal deviation, including any accepted local terrain edit                                  |
| Radius                 | Production validation at least 28 m touring / 45 m GP; proposal screening is slightly stricter                        |
| Width                  | At least 10 m touring / 12 m GP, with edge taper at most 4.5 cm/m                                                     |
| Grid                   | At least 160 m touring / 250 m GP of actual nearly straight, level, unbanked road                                     |
| Grade                  | Comfortable up to 6%; deliberately selected terrain envelope up to 10%                                                |
| Vertical load          | At most 0.40 g crest unloading / 0.65 g additional compression, sampled on centre, tyre and near-edge paths           |
| Bank                   | Beneficial camber, at most 7°, transition at most 0.0025 rad/m; normal designs use much smaller angles                |
| Clearance              | Every non-adjacent crossing checked; width-dependent local exclusion and conservative road/corridor separation        |
| Earthworks             | Corridor-wide sampled RMS up to 4 m gentle / 6 m rolling, maximum residual 16 m, at least 42% natural relief retained |
| Failure delivery       | Explicitly labelled conservative v2 fallback on the original seed's terrain; counted separately from native success   |

The acceleration tests use a curvature-limited reference speed capped at 40 m/s
touring / 52 m/s GP. A reference line can display a faster class-specific estimate,
but it is also limited by tyre-path vertical acceleration. These are game engineering
bounds, not real-world circuit safety certification. Escape rectangles reserve space
for M5; the current corridor spacing is not a detailed high-speed runoff design.

Rhythm validation reads the fitted curve, grouping turns with at least 20 degrees
of accumulated direction change over at least 15 m. Technical touring/GP designs
require at least 6/8 such groups and four alternating direction changes. Mixed
designs require 4/5 groups and two direction changes. Flowing designs allow three
groups. These are minimum brief checks, not a score to maximize corner count.
Conservative fallback delivery explicitly reports when the requested rhythm was
not produced. The visual regression fixture verifies that an otherwise valid
simple loop fails a technical brief.

Reproduce qualification with:

```sh
npm run qualify:m4 -- --count=1000 --workers=6 --compile=all
```

The four named presets are touring/gentle, touring/rolling, GP/gentle and GP/rolling.
Each preset's campaign spans minimum/middle/maximum lengths, all three rhythms,
route alternatives and the optional terrain envelope. It is a stratified campaign,
not an exhaustive Cartesian product. Every accepted case compiles the road and terrain;
native success, fallback delivery and failure are recorded separately. Artifacts
include every recipe, rejection report, fingerprint, metrics, time and mesh size.
The source/dependency hash identifies exactly what was tested.

After qualification, `npm run report:m4` writes the compact checked-in summary and
an interactive/SVG portfolio of **32 distinct seeds**. Eight cases per preset use
fixed disjoint ranges: 0–7, 8–15, 16–23 and 24–31. Selection does not depend on
shape. Maps regenerated for the report must match their qualified fingerprints.
Full case records remain in `artifacts/m4-v2/qualification.json`.

## Scope after M4

The subsequent [M5 implementation](m5.md) supplies trajectory-based run-off guides,
barriers, actual pits/access construction and richer circuit dressing.
M6 expands procedural vehicles and setup
comparisons. M7 supplies inherited rural/urban/airfield road networks, ovals and road/
oval hybrids, extended endurance scale and the separately optional bridge-layer work.
Road graph interfaces and geographic itineraries in M4 do not claim that those M7
families are already implemented. Open rally stages, hill climbs and cliff roads stay
outside the project scope.
