# M7 — inherited roads, speedways and endurance scale

M7 implements stages A–C of the [approved M4/M7 revision](m4-revised-plan.md).
It adds seven v3 circuit families to the existing touring/GP generator. Work is
delivered on `codex/m7-track-families`, then merged and pushed to `development`
for testing under the [repository workflow](workflow.md). M7 is included in the
M0–M8 [v0.2.0 baseline](releases/v0.2.0.md). Racing bridges/tunnels (optional M7-D),
timing, ghosts and race rules were outside this milestone.

M8 subsequently adds those solo sessions, ghosts, rules and the multi-entry circuit
library; see [the M8 guide](m8.md). The measurements below remain M7 evidence.

## Driving and workshop

Open **Circuit workshop** and select a circuit style:

| Style                      | Full-venue target | Construction                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Urban street               | 2.2–6.0 km        | City blocks, diagonal arterials, rounded junctions, close walls, paved junction escapes and primitive buildings  |
| Inherited road / endurance | 4.0–14.0 km       | Rural road graph, contour offsets, long road sectors and a purpose-built technical connector where space permits |
| Mountain road              | 3.5–7.5 km        | Closed climb/ridge/valley loop, bounded grades, protected verges and retained terrain                            |
| Airfield                   | 2.8–7.0 km        | Runway/taxiway axes, broad pavement, apron corners and primitive hangar buildings                                |
| Oval                       | 0.8–4.4 km        | Dedicated oval, tri-oval or asymmetric-end grammar; progressive lane bank, inner apron and outer wall            |
| Road / oval hybrid         | 2.8–6.8 km        | Shared speedway facility with full infield, infield sprint and speedway variants                                 |
| Extended endurance         | 8.0–24.0 km       | Larger inherited-road network venue with long-lap construction and bounded local residency                       |

Length specifies the **full venue**, including when a shorter variant is selected.
The preview reports the actual driven length. Route A/B/C and corner rhythm choose
different closed cycles on the same inherited road graph. Changing the length
changes the physical site scale. Club variants retain the full site's roads and
use a shorter cycle; they do not shrink the car, road width or whole silhouette.

The preview adds road-network/property overlays, provenance, graph and variant
details, construction/transfer budgets and an interactive road cross-section.
Select the stock speed model on an oval/hybrid to compare racing, low, middle and
high lines. Reference speeds include grade, banking and tyre-path crest limits;
they are design estimates, not championship lap-time predictions or AI drivers.
GT/prototype/touring lines use different class margins and speed envelopes.

High/middle/low speedway starts expose the local road normal and individual wheel
loads. Ordinary circuit controls, keyboard/gamepad, tuning, saved cars, live
pitch/roll diagrams and the M6 comparison workbench continue to work. Loading a
circuit retains the active vehicle/setup. Pit starts identify the separate `pit`
branch in telemetry and the HUD. Pit limits/merge guidance remain advisory until M8
adds race control.

## Generation and surface model

`src/track/families` separates synthetic inherited roads from the accepted racing
alignment. Urban blocks/arterials, warped rural/mountain roads and airfield axes
carry fixed edge IDs, origins, widths and property reservations. Connected block
regions supply simple closed graph cycles; candidate alternatives reuse exact road
positions. Junction footprints reserve room for circular fillets before the
periodic C2 curve fit. Rural connectors are explicit new roads with provenance.
All venues are fictional design briefs, rather than replicas or imported maps.

The inherited centreline is only the starting alignment. A periodic, smoothed
grade field with zero net climb is integrated into elevation; the grid is locked
level. Widths follow road/junction/grid roles. Camber magnitudes and same-direction
corner transitions are fitted in physical metres, without masking a completed
profile by instantaneous curvature. The natural height field remains independent
and visible in the preview. Run-off, barriers, pits and scenery then fit the
accepted road. Retained unused roads appear as terrain pavement; only the selected
racing cycle receives the full circuit grading/crossfall treatment.

Speedways use tangent straights and circular ends, including asymmetric radii and
a smooth tri-oval front stretch. Hybrid infields connect to that facility through
designed width and banking transitions. Banking integrates a lane-dependent slope
field; the outer lane can reach 23°, while the inner lane is shallower. A C2 blend
joins it to a 7 m, 2° apron and then flatter terrain. Very short ovals reduce peak
bank to fit safe transitions. This is independent of the touring/GP bank formula.

The shared surface compiler tessellates the complete cross-section. Rendering and
Rapier consume the same Float32 vertices, indices and per-triangle materials,
including lanes, apron, kerbs and terrain. Constrained terrain retains exact road
seams. Detail-point spacing/jitter and axis canonicalisation prevent nearly
collinear floating-point slivers, rather than discarding failed terrain faces.

Validation checks closed cycles, crossings/non-local clearance, fitted radii,
width/taper, grades, lane-bank/rate, tyre-path crest/compression, a level grid,
periodic position/tangent/profile joins and graph provenance. Construction checks
barrier clearance, paved pit branches and property-aware facilities. Invalid seeds
fail with their recipe and reasons; v3 does not silently substitute a seed or use
a radial fallback. The original v1/v2 pipelines and fingerprints remain supported.

## Endurance residency and persistence

- Road samples use up to 2 m longitudinal steps, or 3 m on longer venues, with
  adaptive surface-error subdivision. Terrain spacing is 20/28/38 m by venue size.
- Compiled CPU geometry is retained for a complete venue. Local physics uses a
  260 m window, 360 m retirement hysteresis and caps of 112 surface / 112 obstacle
  tiles. Welded barrier triangles are split without changing their offsets.
- GPU road/terrain buffers are loaded locally and retired. Scenery uses culled,
  lazy primitive instance batches. The full-venue camera uses a coarse overview;
  return to a driving camera to inspect exact contact geometry.
- Nearby progress uses a 48 m spatial grid. Distant queries use a bounds hierarchy
  instead of scanning all lap rows; diagnostics count actual tested segments.
  Main/variant/pit IDs and reserved layer 0 travel with telemetry. All racing
  routes remain non-crossing; no layered localization is claimed.
- Preview cache residency is limited to three results and 48 MiB of typed geometry
  buffers (a single current result is retained even if larger). This is not a
  total browser-memory limit: definitions, temporary construction data, Rapier and
  renderer allocations also consume memory.

**Drive circuit** saves the accepted definition and active variant in IndexedDB.
After reload, use **Restore / import a circuit → Restore saved circuit**. Startup
still opens the proving circuit; restoring is explicit. JSON export includes the
versioned recipe, accepted definition, checksum and evidence. Large exports use
compact JSON so a 24 km circuit remains importable. Import accepts files or pasted
JSON, bounds its data, verifies the checksum and revalidates/compiles the accepted
geometry without regenerating the route. Saved car data is independent. A full
multi-entry circuit library belongs to M8.

## Qualification and reproduction

Run from the milestone branch (use `npm.cmd` in PowerShell):

```sh
npm test -- --maxWorkers=2
npm run build
npm run qualify:m7 -- --count=1000 --construction=32
npm run qualify:m7:driving
```

The delivered run passed **7,000/7,000 planning checks**, **224/224 complete
constructions** and **31/31 driving laps**, with zero seed substitutions or
failures. All committed M7 reports match runtime source hash `0c3c2dff67917ddb`.

The generation campaign distinguishes **7,000 planning checks** (1,000 per family)
from **224 complete mesh/venue constructions** (32 per family). Construction covers
length boundaries, all oval shapes, terrain/rhythm/layout variants, pits on/off,
raised/low kerbs and scenery themes. It checks finite/upward/non-degenerate shared
triangles, material cardinality, nearby-leg localization, archives and class lines.
Planning is not equivalent to compiling 7,000 meshes.

Full-lap qualification drives FF/FR/MR/AWD references on all seven families,
including 24 km, and a procedural FR stock car on low/middle/high oval lanes.
The driver deliberately uses conservative caps and lower corner acceleration for
the MR sport reference. Those checks establish contact/streaming compatibility;
they do not certify every tuning combination or maximum racing speed. The tyres
and suspension can still unload wheels under aggressive inputs.

Open `/m7-benchmark.html` for a separate **visible-browser**, fixed 1920×1080
measurement using the production renderer, generation worker and physics. It
measures street, oval and 24 km endurance venues after warm-up and reports frame
intervals, simulation/render submission time, geometry, collider residency and
available JS heap data. It does not claim GPU-memory bytes or portable performance
on other hardware. Run with other intensive workloads idle. The named desktop is
Ryzen 5 5600X, 32 GiB, RTX 3060; the report also records the renderer exposed by the
actual browser.

Copy its JSON into `artifacts/m7/desktop-benchmark.json`, add the current `sourceHash`
and hardware/measurement notes, then run `npm run report:m7`. The report command
requires matching hashes and passing campaigns. Hashing covers sorted `src/**/*.ts`
and CSS plus `package-lock.json`; generation timings from parallel Node
workers are separate from foreground browser frame measurements.

Measured desktop sample (26 October 2026): all three venues delivered about 100 fps
at 1920×1080, with 10.1 ms p95 frame intervals and the actual RTX 3060 ANGLE renderer.
Render-submission p95 was 1.4 ms street/oval and 2.9 ms endurance. Peak exposed JS
heap was approximately 71/100/173 MiB respectively. Each case measured 720 frames
after 180 warm-up frames and drove about 152 m; these are short foreground samples,
not rendered full-lap or worst-case performance certification. Browser/display
delivery was capped near 100 Hz. GPU/WASM memory is outside the JS heap readings.
