# M8 — solo sessions, ghosts and the circuit library

M8 completes the planned solo practice/time-trial feature set on
`codex/m8-solo-sessions`, integrated and tested on `development`. The user approved
promotion of M0–M8 and the tyre-feedback follow-up to `main` as
[v0.2.0](releases/v0.2.0.md). The earlier v0.1.0 M0–M5 tag remains available.
See the [branch workflow](workflow.md).

The [first-pass workbench follow-up](ui-workbench-first-pass.md) updates the linked
M8 qualification records for development source `8565dd5b29ffc0a7`. Release results
and hashes quoted below remain historical evidence for their stated releases;
the report files identify the newer implementation actually measured. Timing is
now in the compact working toolbar, test environments are under Tests, and general
presentation preferences are in Settings.

## Start a session

Load any supported circuit and choose **Start time trial**. The car resets to its
grid, with a visible chequered timing line ahead. Drive across that line to start
the clock. A valid finish starts the next flying lap immediately. **Practice** in
**Session & records** provides lap information without awarding personal bests.
The tuning workbench and live wheel/pitch/roll diagrams remain available.

The timing strip shows the current lap, sector, simulation-time clock, personal
best and live delta to the reference ghost. Passed sector times include deltas
against the personal best; last-lap splits remain visible until the next sector.
**Session & records** contains recent laps, validity reasons, reference settings,
import/export and presentation controls.

## Local solo rules

- Three sectors and ordered intermediate checkpoints use the circuit's true 3D
  distance. There are at least 12 gates, spaced at most about 120 m apart. Plane
  crossings are interpolated between 120 Hz physics samples, directional and
  bounded by the road width and a 2.5 m local height envelope.
- At least one of the four tyre-centre projections must remain within the white
  racing edge. Kerbs, tarmac run-off and oval aprons are outside this region.
  A 10 cm geometric tolerance avoids boundary noise; this is an explicit game
  rule, not a claim to reproduce any championship's sporting regulations.
- All four tyres outside, reversed/skipped gates, sustained wrong-way driving,
  pit use, resets, impulses, car/setup changes, pauses, focus loss and long browser
  suspensions invalidate the current lap. Finish it to begin a clean lap or start
  a new attempt. Opening a modal pauses driving and invalidates an active lap.
- Pit speed limits are enforced on the separate pit lane after the chassis
  centre leaves the racing road. Shared merge pavement is not a pit offence.
  A blue pit flag and yellow off-track/wrong-way warnings support safe rejoining.
  Pit use requires an out-lap; overspeed is also recorded. There are no AI rivals,
  championship penalties, damage or multiplayer race control in this solo build.

The session engine owns timing and validity independently of the vehicle. An
external controller can supply the same `VehicleControls` for qualification or
future AI without owning lap state.

## Personal bests and ghosts

Each result is keyed by circuit geometry/physical facilities, route variant, car,
mechanical setup, assists, rules and physics version. Paint and non-solid scenery
do not change comparison eligibility. A build fingerprint covers the vehicle,
physics, race logic, shared road/compiler code and dependency lockfile. Restart a
development server after editing those files; production builds stamp the current
fingerprint automatically. Different versions do not silently inherit a best.

For the v0.2.0 promotion, the package/lockfile version bump changes that fingerprint
to `3915c9277118cf78`, even though the approved runtime/physics source is unchanged.
Earlier development records keep their old keys and are not deleted; their ghosts
are incompatible with the release key. See the [upgrade notes](releases/v0.2.0.md).

Ghosts capture body position/quaternion, each wheel's suspension and rotation,
front steering and driver controls at 20 Hz, with exact interpolated timing-line
endpoints. Playback interpolates poses, never creates a collision body and does
not promise deterministic input replay across physics/browser versions. Ghost
visibility and opacity are adjustable.

The working recorder has a fixed approximately 6.6 MiB buffer and a 60 minute lap
limit. A new valid timed personal best stores its ghost in IndexedDB. Up to 80
result groups and 12 ghosts within 64 MiB are retained; older result metadata can
remain after its ghost is retired. Each group retains eight recent laps.

**Export best / reference JSON** backs up the current reference. Import accepts a
file or pasted JSON, checks version/checksum, pose bounds/order, sector totals and
the complete compatibility key. Imported references never replace a locally
earned personal best. This is local comparison data with integrity checks, not a
signed leaderboard or anti-cheat system. Storage failures are shown explicitly;
the current in-memory session can still be exported.

## Circuit library

**Circuit library** saves accepted generated circuit definitions with their recipe
and active variant. Name, favourite, search, rename and load entries without
replacing the active car. Loading recompiles and validates the saved definition;
it does not generate a fresh interpretation of the seed. The proving circuit
remains built in. **Add last M7 saved circuit** preserves access to the previous
single-circuit store.

Import/export supports individual accepted circuit JSON and complete active
libraries. Duplicate accepted recipe/definition entries are updated, and names
and import sizes are bounded. The library holds 24 active circuits and up to 24
recoverable removed entries within a combined 32 MiB budget. **Move to trash** is
recoverable through **Show → Trash → Restore selected**. There is no automatic
permanent purge. Browser storage is local to the origin, including its port;
export before moving between origins or clearing browser data.

## Presentation

- Chase look-ahead is adjustable; the camera follows translation without
  frame-rate-dependent car/camera lag. Static-world raycasts shorten an obstructed
  chase boom and keep it above local terrain. A bonnet view blends body roll
  separately for comfort; top, orbit and whole-circuit views remain available.
- Contact slip power drives skid marks and smoke. Grass, gravel and sand can emit
  coloured dust. Pools are bounded at 1,800 marks and 80 particles, cleared on
  reset/circuit changes and frozen when simulation time stops.
- The tyre-feedback follow-up (`codex/m8-tyre-feedback-sensitivity`) doubles
  rubber-mark onset from 1.4 to 2.8 kW per contact, approximately halving its
  sensitivity without fading the marks. Tyre squeal uses 20% of the original
  response in ordinary partial slip, then smoothly recovers its original maximum
  for substantial wheelspin/lockup or sideways sliding. The recovery is weighted
  by each grounded tyre's slip power. This changes slip response, not the sound
  volume control; engine, impact and loose-surface rolling/dust feedback retain
  their previous settings. Physics and existing result/ghost compatibility remain
  unchanged.
- **Driving sound** starts synthesized engine/load, tyre scrub, surface/kerb
  rumble and impact feedback from a user gesture. It uses no microphone, network
  or asset download. Pause/focus loss silences it; volume is adjustable.
- High, balanced and economy quality settings provide a resolution/shadow
  fallback. Economy disables shadows and effects, preserving the physics and
  race rules. Circuit driving hides the large workshop heading to keep the road
  and map readable. Timing and diagrams fit the desktop layout.

## Qualification and reproduction

```sh
npm test -- --maxWorkers=2
npm run build
npm run qualify:m8
npm run qualify:m8:stress
```

The physical campaign contains 25 conservative timed laps: FR on all seven M7
families including 24 km; all four reference cars and all eleven procedural
archetypes on the authored circuit; and a stock car on low/middle/high oval lanes.
Every case must pass the production gates/limits, record a best and ghost, export
and import it under the same key, and sample finite playback poses. This is not
every car/track/tuning combination or maximum racing speed certification. M6/M7
generation algorithms and construction are unchanged; their original campaigns
remain historical evidence.

The v0.2.0 release repeated the physical campaign: **25/25 laps** passed
with no failures, at runtime source hash `05ddbc2d8655f19b` and
physics/rules build `3915c9277118cf78`. The full regression suite passed
**244/244 tests**, including partial-slip sensitivity, progressive peak recovery,
both longitudinal/lateral full slip, airborne contacts and rubber-mark onset.
The earlier tyre-feedback campaign remains on `codex/m8-tyre-feedback-sensitivity`
at source hash `eda0572759b0d72e` / build `3b519332fd726112`; only release metadata
changes the current fingerprints.

`/m8-lap-test.html` runs the production app with a conservative scripted controller
after its normal **Start time trial** button. Its results/library use an isolated
qualification database. This verifies actual HUD, simulation, ghost and IndexedDB
integration without populating the normal user's result store.

`/m8-benchmark.html` measures three visible-browser 1920×1080 rendering samples with
the production renderer, four-wheel rig, timing/session logic, working recorder,
effects and a synthetic trailing ghost for render load. It excludes the workbench
DOM; actual recorded ghost playback and full laps are separate tests. Generation
and setup precede 180 warm-up and 720 measured frames per venue. Record the actual
GPU, visibility, frame intervals and exposed JS heap. GPU/WASM memory is not
included in JS heap readings. Short frame samples do not certify worst-case laps.

On the Ryzen 5 5600X / 32 GiB / RTX 3060 desktop, the delivered visible IAB
samples measured **100.0 / 100.0 / 100.0 fps** for street/oval/endurance, with
**10.1 ms p95 frame intervals**. Release simulation p95 was 0.9/0.8/1.0 ms and
render submission p95 was 1.9/1.7/2.3 ms. Exposed peak JS heap was approximately
86/115/192 MiB. Delivery was capped near 100 Hz; other desktop activity was not
controlled. All three sessions were running with their fixed recorder allocated.

The original M8 production-app browser campaign exercised 19 session/library/
presentation checks, including result/ghost reload and library restoration; that
evidence remains on `codex/m8-solo-sessions`. The tyre-feedback follow-up completed
another **61.198 s valid physical lap** with sound/effects enabled and seven focused
browser checks, including preserved volume, effects, compatible personal best and
ghost, working sound activation/muting and no console errors. Numerical response
and mark onset are verified by the regression tests; subjective sound/handling
feel was not assessed. Qualification uses an isolated database; the normal user's
records are not populated by the scripted driver.

The v0.2.0 release reran eight browser smoke checks on its new build key: a valid
61.198 s physical lap with a validated 1,225-pose ghost; best/reference restoration
after reload; rejection of a pre-release ghost; preserved effects/volume; and
restoration of the saved 24.05 km favourite circuit without replacing the FR car.
No console errors were reported. These release checks and the renderer/physical
campaigns above match the current release source hash. The three-independent-world
CPU sample measured 1.09 ms p95 for two 120 Hz steps per measured frame.

The separate Node stress sample profiles three independent vehicle/world
instances, two 120 Hz steps per measured 60 Hz frame. It is CPU-only and does not
include a shared-world AI grid or inter-car collision cost.
