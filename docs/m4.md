## Workflow and reproducibility

Choose a seed (up to 64 characters), **Touring** or **Grand Prix**, **Gentle terrain**
or **Rolling hills**, and lap length. Touring accepts 1,800–3,200 m; GP accepts
3,200–5,200 m, in 100 m steps. These are horizontal alignment targets; the displayed
lap distance includes elevation. A seed, family, terrain choice, length and generator
version form a complete recipe. Whitespace is trimmed and other input is bounded.

**Preview seed** produces a topographic base-terrain map, road layout, reserved
paddock, elevation trace, six dimensions and a nine-check report. The current driving
scene pauses while the workshop is open. Editing a recipe marks its displayed preview
stale and disables **Drive circuit** until the new settings have been generated.
**Shuffle seed** supplies a new seed and previews it. **Copy recipe** copies JSON
containing the versioned recipe, fingerprint, validation report and rejected attempts.

**Drive circuit** loads a validated preview, resets to its grid and preserves the
current car/tuning. New starts include the grid, middle sector, highest point and
tightest bend. The scene map/elevation and overview camera fit the new layout. R,
reference-car changes, chassis rebuilds and pad switches retain the active circuit.
The workshop remembers the last loaded recipe in browser storage; startup still uses
the authored proving circuit. **Load M3 proving circuit** returns to the diagnostics.

Random streams are local and seeded. Layout, terrain and cosmetic placement have
separate streams. Candidate data is quantised before compilation to remove insignificant
numeric differences; its fingerprint includes the final definition, profiles, site and
generator version. Repeating the same v1 recipe reproduces its accepted attempt and
fallback decision. Future incompatible generation changes must use a new recipe version.

## Generation pipeline

1. Three octave bands of seeded quintic value noise produce broad terrain. Value
   noise is the Perlin-equivalent chosen here; amplitudes are bounded, with more
   relief for rolling hills. It does not add high-frequency bumps to the driving road.
2. Ordered sectors around a reserved site compose a long collinear starting straight,
   sweepers, tightening bends and an infield left/right or double-apex complex. Sector
   count, angles, radii, aspect ratio and complex placement vary. A periodic cubic
   B-spline closes the entire route with C2 continuity, including its seam. The M3
   authored layout is not used as a procedural template.
3. The alignment scales to the requested length. Six candidate headings are compared
   on the same seeded terrain for grading/grid cost. Terrain samples are filtered into
   periodic elevation profiles, with a level grid and slope/vertical-curvature limits.
   The road follows broad terrain features; it is not draped over every noise contour.
4. Independent edge profiles provide gradual widening/narrowing and a wider grid.
   Positive corner camber derives from curvature; straights stay unbanked and crown
   remains independent. Banking strength is reduced if its transition or vertical
   acceleration would exceed the design budget.
5. Actual curvature runs determine semantic section labels and tapered inside-corner
   kerbs, with shorter outer entry/exit strips on longer bends. Mixed tarmac, grass,
   gravel and sand run-offs retain M3's materials and wheel physics.
6. The shared compiler grades the corridor into seeded outside/infield terrain over
   an 80 m transition. Radial rings add interior terrain detail without holes or
   mismatched road boundaries. A level footprint beside the grid is reserved for
   future pits/paddock; trees avoid it. Terrain tint varies subtly with height.
7. Validate geometry, compile the exact render/collision triangles, validate the
   transferred Float32 topology, and return buffers for preview/loading.

The worker performs terrain fitting, validation and mesh compilation. Main-thread
hydration restores alignment/frame methods and a 48 m spatial grid for nearest-road
telemetry; distant queries fall back to a complete nearest-segment search. No second
independent road mesh or collision heightfield is constructed.

Start-line checks and direction arrows share two paint batches. Trees are instanced;
decoration draw calls do not grow with arrow or tree count as laps get longer.

## Validation and failure handling

| Check                 | Current v1 bounds                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Closure and clearance | No sampled segment crossings; at least 24 m between road edges of nonlocal sections, allowing both run-off corridors         |
| Corner radius         | At least 32 m touring / 55 m GP                                                                                              |
| Grade                 | At most 6% centreline grade; fitted profiles target 5%                                                                       |
| Vertical transitions  | At most 0.40 g unloading / 0.65 g extra compression on centre, tyre and near-edge paths                                      |
| Corner banking        | Beneficial camber only; at most 7°, with transition at most 0.0025 rad/m (about 0.14°/m)                                     |
| Width/taper           | At least 10 m touring / 12 m GP; each edge tapers at most 4.5 cm/m                                                           |
| Starting straight     | At least 160 m touring / 250 m GP of contiguous level, nearly straight road around the grid, plus a reserved level footprint |
| Closing seam          | Matching periodic position, tangent, height, widths and bank                                                                 |
| Surface mesh          | Finite upward triangles and shared render/collision topology; folded road/terrain rejected                                   |

Vertical checks use a curvature-limited reference speed (0.8 g cornering), capped at
38 m/s touring / 50 m/s GP. They are geometry checks at these reference speeds, not a
promise that every setup can take every bend flat out. Clearance checks exclude 100 m
of neighbouring station for proximity, while intersection checks only exclude adjacent
segments. Geometry is sampled every 4 m; the compiler independently rejects local
folds using its denser adaptive sampling and the actual Float32 output.

Generation tries up to four candidates, retaining the original recipe and each rejection
reason. If all fail, it validates and compiles a conservative closed layout for that
family on the same seeded terrain. Fallback use is explicit in the preview status,
load caption and expandable attempt report. If that also fails, the user gets an error
and the active circuit remains available. `sweep-0 / touring / rolling / 1,800 m` is a
regression fixture: four candidates fail minimum radius, then a valid fallback loads.

Cancellation terminates the worker, including synchronous mesh compilation. Starting
another job cancels the previous one; stale results are ignored. Worker errors release
the job and allow another preview. Scene geometry is prepared before changing physics;
a failed collision build removes any partial body/colliders. Successful swaps dispose
old mesh/instance/material resources and remove old colliders.

## Verification

The complete **98-test** suite passes, with production TypeScript/Vite build checks.
M4 adds 27 checks covering deterministic geometry/terrain, multiple seeds at both
families' length bounds and relief settings, safe fallback provenance, rejection
reasons, actual buffer transfer/hydration, road/mesh height and material agreement,
spatial lookup, cancellation/stale jobs, partial-build rollback, collider replacement,
GPU-resource disposal, and existing test-pad/reset/rebuild behaviour.

Full laps use a test-only follower with the FF/FR touring references at 15/18 m/s and
MR/AWD GP references at 22 m/s. They cross the closing seam, stay within 1 m of the
centreline, keep four contacts for more than 99.5% of samples, and keep peak total
support below 1.8 vehicle weights. The player still drives through keyboard/gamepad.
A separate 48-recipe sweep (both families, both relief choices, three lengths, four
seeds) passed all cases, including explicitly reported conservative fallbacks.

Browser smoke checks cover preview/generate/edit/load, both circuit families, an
invalidated preview, cancellation, fallback reporting, overview, generated starts,
actual driving, per-wheel telemetry and returning to the authored venue/pads. Physical
gamepad hardware and the named 1080p/60 reference machine remain unverified.

## Deliberate limits

This is the first touring/GP generator. Its reserved radial site permits varied
concave complexes but excludes routes that would fold the outside or infield rings.
It does not search arbitrary terrain networks, create overpasses, or generate street,
oval or open-road layouts. A conservative fallback can have a simpler rhythm.

Run-off width/materials and automatic curvature kerbs are useful M3-based references;
speed/trajectory-derived escape envelopes, detailed braking-zone design, barrier
collisions, pits/facilities, grandstands and richer scenery remain M5. Trees are
cosmetic primitives. Race timing, limits enforcement, AI and broad car generation
remain their planned milestones. These are game design bounds, not homologated tracks.
