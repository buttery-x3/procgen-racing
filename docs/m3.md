> **Branch policy:** `main` holds the M0–M5 `v0.1.0` baseline. Each future

> to remote `development` on completion for testing. Later releases to `main`

Implemented 25 October 2026. The app opens on **3D circuit**, with the original M1/M2
pads available in the same toolbar. The circuit is authored; M4 remains responsible
for procedural route/terrain generation and its design search.

## Delivered venue

Ridge is a closed circuit approximately 662 m long. Its road varies from 9.5 to
15 m wide and rises from 1.2 to 8.6 m above the venue datum. It includes an uphill
straight, banked summit, changing-radius descent, narrowing esses, gravel bend,
return crests and a widening exit. Positive corner curvature banks the left edge
down; opposite curvature reverses the bank. Straight sections remain unbanked.

Low kerbs use an actual 55 mm rounded cross-section, 0.9 m width and 8 m longitudinal
tapers, with red/white paint. They appear in authored entry/apex/exit bands.
Tarmac, gravel, sand and grass run-offs are part of the driving mesh. Primitive
trees, start-line checks, edge paint and direction arrows make the venue readable.

Seven Test starts provide repeatable comparisons: start straight, ridge climb,
banked summit, downhill compound, left-wheel kerb, asphalt/gravel and sand escape.
Selecting a start resets and aligns the chassis to the local surface; R, drop
tests and car changes retain that selected location. The selector releases input
focus so driving can begin immediately.

The scene includes a route map, elevation trace, station distance, grade, banking,
width and road height. Circuit overview allows inspection of the entire loop.
The Tyres tab reports each wheel's actual contact material alongside slip and RPM,
with a colour legend. Existing tuning, world/road attitude diagrams, force
overlays and interpolated cameras remain available.

## Compiler contract

- `track/types.ts` defines the authored alignment, periodic elevation/width/bank/
  crown profiles, semantic sections, shoulders, kerb zones and test starts.
- `Alignment` evaluates a periodic cubic B-spline in the horizontal plane, with
  C2 continuity at every knot and the closing seam. A dense arc table maps horizontal
  station to the spline. Elevation and left/right widths are independent quintic
  profiles. Banking strength follows authored keys and curvature; crown is separate.
- The frame uses the horizontal tangent, a consistently oriented lateral vector
  and world-up grade reference. It does not use a Frenet normal, so straights and
  inflections do not flip the frame. This construction is appropriate for the
  bounded grades of a closed circuit. Road normals come from compiled triangles.
- The surface function includes road bank/crown, raised kerbs, verge, shoulder and
  run-off. Crossfall continues smoothly off the edge and levels over four metres.
  Inside run-off width is constrained by corner radius to avoid folding offsets.
- Longitudinal sampling adapts to road-edge deviation, heading/bank geometry and
  a 1.5 m maximum step; the edge midpoint error target is 12 mm. Transverse vertices
  explicitly represent road, paint margins, kerb shape and material boundaries.
- The graded outside terrain expands to a finite site boundary; its height blends
  from the outer verge to low-frequency analytic undulation. Constrained polygon
  triangulation fills the infield using the exact inner-verge boundary. Both
  circuit directions are supported. The outside grading requires a suitable site
  centre and outline; folds are rejected rather than emitted. General terrain
  optimization and earthworks are M4 work.
- The compiler emits shared Float32 vertices, triangle indices, material IDs and
  display colours. Render meshes expand these exact triangles for sharp paint;
  collision uses the same coordinates and topology. Paint has no separate raised
  collision ribs. An explicit cumulative 3D distance table is distinct from
  horizontal station. This is a geometry diagnostic, not lap timing.
- The venue uses 458 sampled rows and approximately 21,500 triangles in 11 chunks,
  including the infield. Geometry is compiled once and shared by renderer and
  physics; switching pads toggles bodies and visuals without recompiling.

## Contact physics and stability

Each wheel ray reads its collider's compiled material and actual hit normal. In
the installed Rapier 0.21.0 build, mesh ray queries reproduced a triangle feature
ID of zero at different known triangles. The implementation therefore groups each
chunk's existing triangles by material and assigns metadata to their colliders.
It preserves the compiler's geometry and obtains material IDs directly from the
collider handle. Queries do not guess surface type from car position or centreline
distance. Internal-edge handling is enabled within each mesh collider.

| Surface        | Grip multiplier | Rolling resistance coefficient |
| -------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Asphalt        | 1.00            | 0.006                          |
| Kerb           | 0.88            | 0.012                          |
| Tarmac run-off | 0.96            | 0.008                          |
| Grass          | 0.55            | 0.035                          |
| Gravel         | 0.48            | 0.065                          |
| Sand           | 0.38            | 0.100                          |

These are handling references. Surface grip scales each wheel's load-sensitive
combined-slip budget; rolling resistance adds load-proportional resisting wheel
torque. Loose surfaces do not simulate deformable soil. The gravel diagnostic
shoulder intentionally abuts asphalt so one side can use each material.

Stationary hold previously cancelled every contact-point velocity in one 120 Hz
step. On crowned road its roll/yaw corrections could fight each other, alternating
wheel loads by roughly 570 N. Finite contact damping removes that oscillation;
settled crowned-road load variation is now below 1 N in the regression check.
Physical spring/damper forces, free chassis motion and grip limits are retained.

## Verification

The 71-test project suite includes 23 M3 checks for:

- Seam continuity, upward triangle winding, finite geometry, adaptive sampling,
  useful width/elevation variation and beneficial banking.
- Both circuit directions and rejection of open profiles/folded offsets.
- Ray/road height agreement within 15 mm at every sample row and midpoint,
  including chunk joins and the closing seam, with the expected asphalt material.
- Static grade/bank attitude, stable crown hold, independent raised kerb contacts,
  all four reference cars with asphalt/gravel on opposite sides, sand resistance
  isolated in neutral, and recovery after drops/rebuilds/pad switches.
- FF, FR, MR and AWD full laps at an 11 m/s target using a test-only path follower.
  All four keep four contacts; measured maximum centreline error was below 0.33 m
  and peak total support about 1.05 times vehicle weight. The tests exercise pitch
  above 6° and roll above 5° and cross the closing seam.
- Kerb traversal and its taper at 8 and 20 m/s (29 and 72 km/h), retaining four
  contacts without excessive load spikes.

The follower exists only in tests; player controls remain keyboard/gamepad. Strict
TypeScript checking and the production build remain required. Browser smoke checks
cover overview, test starts, actual driving, world/road attitude, independent
surface telemetry, switching back to the pads and reset/camera behaviour.

Wheel contact still uses rays and virtual wheels. Wider tyre footprints, extreme
kerbs, high-speed jumps and the full vehicle range need further validation as
content expands. Automatic kerb/run-off design, barriers/facilities, general site
generation, race logic and weather remain their later milestones. The authored
surface is a validation venue, not a certified real-world circuit.
