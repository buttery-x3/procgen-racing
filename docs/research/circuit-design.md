# Circuit design research — M4 revision

> **Branch policy:** `main` holds the M0–M5 `v0.1.0` baseline. Each future
> milestone uses its own branch from `development`, then is merged and pushed
> to remote `development` on completion for testing. Later releases to `main`
> require an explicit request. See the [release workflow](../workflow.md).

Reviewed 25 October 2026. This is a study of circuit geometry, setting and design
history, not a claim that every venue below appears on the current F1 calendar.
The sources are circuit operators, series, teams, governing bodies and the original
project. Published corner counts use different conventions; they are not comparable
complexity scores. Historical configurations are explicitly distinguished from
present layouts. Proposed generator rules below are our design inferences.

## Findings for design, rather than imitation

1. **There are several scales of structure.** Whole-site route arrangement, sector
   rhythm, corner sequences, individual curvature transitions and cross-sections
   all contribute. Randomizing only individual corner radii misses the larger scales.
2. **The site has a history or purpose.** Public roads connect places; airfields have
   inherited axes; a modern venue has a facility plan; a terrain circuit negotiates
   ridges and valleys. Noise gives heights, but does not provide those reasons.
3. **Elevation is correlated with the sequence.** A summit technical section differs
   from a fast valley sweep even with the same plan-view radius. This does not justify
   accidentally placing blind crests in high-speed braking zones.
4. **Complexity is not wiggliness.** Long straights, long-radius corners and recovery
   sections are part of a varied lap. Deliberately simple courses must remain possible.
5. **Real geometry exceeds our initial comfortable settings.** A global 6% grade cap
   cannot represent the cited steep features. Conversely, copying a published maximum
   grade does not establish safe vertical curvature, visibility or suspension loads.
6. **Family labels overlap.** GP, touring and endurance describe use as well as design.
   Prefer separate site origin, layout character, vehicle envelope and venue scale.
7. **Routes and usable land must be solved together.** Adjacent legs may not cross,
   yet still leave no space for escape areas, barriers, access or grading. A nominal
   centreline separation is not a runoff design.

The [2026 FIA Appendix O](https://api.fia.com/system/files/documents/appendix_o_2026_published_23122025.pdf),
Article 7, is a design reference for new permanent circuits, not a universal preset
for every historic venue. It addresses width and gradual transitions, grid geometry,
grade/crossfall, protection and pit access. Its 12 m width guidance is not permission
to force every historic or street circuit into the same width. Our generated venues
are game designs, with explicit vehicle-envelope checks, not homologation claims.

## Re-reading drift-minigame

Source pinned and rechecked at
[`641ddddacc167f4d1ff4fbff3df93ca6d40dabb1`](https://github.com/buttery-x3/drift-minigame/tree/641ddddacc167f4d1ff4fbff3df93ca6d40dabb1).

[`TrackGenerator.ts`](https://github.com/buttery-x3/drift-minigame/blob/641ddddacc167f4d1ff4fbff3df93ca6d40dabb1/src/track/TrackGenerator.ts)
constructs a chain of actual straights and constant-radius corners. It samples
turn signs, angles and tight/wide radii, places one or two long straights, and biases
the remaining net turn toward one full winding. Two closing corners are reserved.
[`TrackGeometry.ts`](https://github.com/buttery-x3/drift-minigame/blob/641ddddacc167f4d1ff4fbff3df93ca6d40dabb1/src/track/TrackGeometry.ts)
solves those with a tangent-continuous biarc. Candidates with unsuitable closure,
overlap or insufficient spacing are discarded. Up to 420 attempts are searched;
the score rewards hairpins, both turn directions and sweep variance, penalizing a
disproportionately long closing section.

That explains the stronger silhouettes: route growth can reverse direction and fold
back without preserving angular order around a centre. It is not a convex-hull or
radial-noise generator. The original implementation plan correctly identified this
foundation, but M4 v1 substituted a more restrictive method.

Retain analytical poses, independent signed turns, seeded candidate search,
inspectable segments and bounded closure as a proposal mechanism. Do not retain
flatness, abrupt curvature jumps at straight/arc joins, constant road width, oil
hazards, the global turn/size limits or a quality score dominated by hairpin count.
Its biarc is G1 (position/tangent continuous), not a ready-made curvature-continuous
driving alignment. A long final connector remains an important source of bias.

## Alternatives and decision

| Method                                                        | Useful property                                                                                 | Failure mode                                                                                 | Decision                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Radial sectors + spline                                       | Cheap closure, easy radial terrain fill                                                         | Deep folded routes excluded by construction                                                  | Keep v1 only for compatibility/regression; stop using it as the core method                  |
| Drift-style segment chain                                     | Strong shape variety, explicit straights/radii, incremental rejection                           | Low acceptance as sequences grow; closure and hairpin scoring bias; no site context          | Implemented as the first experimental proposal engine                                        |
| Terrain-first shortest/least-cost route                       | Can connect site features and existing roads                                                    | Finds the cheapest road, not necessarily an engaging race; grid zigzags and closure problems | Use for corridor proposals between strategic anchors, not as the whole circuit generator     |
| Arrange straights first, then fit corners                     | Useful for overtaking-oriented modern venues and inherited roads                                | Forces every family into straight/corner alternation; connectors consume leftover space      | One proposal mode, with corner-sequence reservations and a closure budget                    |
| Fixed library of whole outlines                               | Easy to make a few attractive previews                                                          | Recognizable repeated templates, same problem in another form                                | Use authored examples as tests only, not the source of all seeds                             |
| **Site graph + sequence grammar + constrained curve fitting** | Separates geography, lap rhythm and mathematical continuity; supports multiple proposal engines | More design and search work; needs non-radial terrain meshing                                | **Recommended production direction**; see the [revised delivery plan](../m4-revised-plan.md) |

This is a hybrid of the reference generator's local geometry and a new strategic
site/route layer. It is not a commitment to put straights first everywhere.
