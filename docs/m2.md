## Vehicle and tyre model

Coordinates are right-handed: +Y up, +Z forward, **+X vehicle left**. Wheel order
is FL, FR, RL, RR. Input steering is positive to the right, so wheel/body yaw is
negative. A shared coordinate helper defines wheel sides, Ackermann angles,
signed forward speed and the centred chase pose. Tests catch the M1 steering and
camera regressions.

Each wheel has angular velocity, rotational inertia, integrated spin and relaxed
longitudinal/lateral force. Its slip ratio is
`(omega * radius - contactSpeed) / max(abs(contactSpeed), abs(omega * radius), 1.5)`.
The denominator is regularized near zero speed. In forward travel, positive slip
means wheelspin and negative slip means braking; signs reverse when reversing.
Slip angle uses the wheel's own contact-point lateral and longitudinal velocities.

Normalized longitudinal slip and lateral slip share a combined-slip curve: a
smooth rise to a peak followed by a broad sliding plateau. Force capacity depends
on normal load, tyre friction, per-contact surface grip and load sensitivity.
Acceleration, braking and cornering consume the same force budget. Wheel torque
integrates engine/differential torque, tyre reaction and non-overshooting brake
torque. A bounded contact impulse and force relaxation stabilize low-speed motion;
the solver applies forces at contact points rather than replacing body velocity.
Airborne wheels can spin but produce no road force.

The split-grip experiment gives the physical left side 22% of the normal surface
grip. Driving outside the apron uses 55% grip. These are diagnostic material
queries; M3 replaces them with compiled road/terrain contact metadata.

## Powertrain, brakes and assists

- A simplified engine torque curve, inertia, idle control, rev limiter, engine
  braking and slipping launch clutch drive the selected wheels. No engine stalling
  or manual clutch pedal is required.
- Six forward gears have road, close or long spacing and a live final drive.
  Automatic shifts have a cooldown and short torque interruption. Sequential
  shifting is also available. R/N/D selection works in either transmission mode;
  direction changes are rejected above 0.5 m/s in the opposite direction, including
  attempts to bypass the guard through neutral.
- Open axle differentials split torque equally, allowing independent speeds. LSDs
  transfer bounded torque toward the slower wheel. Locked axles constrain equal
  wheel angular speeds. AWD adds an adjustable front/rear split and open/LSD/locked
  centre coupling; a locked centre constrains axle mean speeds. Coupling conserves
  total wheel angular momentum and dissipates relative rotation energy. Wheel
  inertias are currently equal.
- Service brakes have adjustable total strength and front bias. ABS independently
  modulates wheel brake torque using grip capacity and locking slip. The rear
  handbrake bypasses ABS. Stationary brake hold is optional and grip-limited.
- TC reduces engine demand from excess driven-wheel slip. Stability applies
  bounded yaw torque; countersteer adds at most 0.2 rad. Both require road contacts.
  Speed-sensitive steering reduces available steering angle at speed. All assists
  can be reduced or disabled; accessible/balanced/direct/drift bundles set their
  individual controls. The automatic launch clutch and engine idle control remain
  part of the base powertrain.
- Drag and optional speed-squared downforce apply forces to the body and load its
  suspension. Stability torque is a deliberate semi-arcade aid, not a simulation
  of hydraulic individual-wheel ESC hardware.

## Reference cars and workbench

| Reference  | Layout                         | Mass     | Front static weight | Peak engine torque |
| ---------- | ------------------------------ | -------- | ------------------- | ------------------ |
| FF compact | Front engine / front drive     | 1,120 kg | 62%                 | 180 Nm             |
| FR coupe   | Front engine / rear drive      | 1,250 kg | 54%                 | 300 Nm             |
| MR sport   | Mid engine / rear drive        | 1,180 kg | 43%                 | 370 Nm             |
| AWD sedan  | Front engine / all-wheel drive | 1,480 kg | 57%                 | 410 Nm             |

These are original handling references, not calibrated replicas. Layout changes
mass distribution, physical COM, driven wheels and reference tuning. Their simple
body/cabin proportions and colours identify them; the full procedural archetype
builder is now delivered in [M6](m6.md), alongside the full tuning/comparison workbench.

The existing suspension and chassis tabs are joined by Drivetrain, Tyres and
Assists. Drivetrain exposes the engine, transmission, diffs, AWD split, brakes and
aero. Tyres includes a live four-wheel slip/angle/RPM table. The gear/RPM HUD,
direction buttons and actual intervention badges stay visible while driving.
Orthographic contact colours distinguish slip, ABS, ground contact and unloading.

Steering now follows A/left and D/right in both scene and diagrams. V toggles
reverse/drive; throttle accelerates in the selected direction, and brake always
brakes. Q/E shift; Shift holds the rear handbrake. Standard gamepad mapping is in
the README and in-app guide. Chase is centred behind the projected vehicle
heading, independent of body pitch/roll.

Save/compare and undo include every M2 parameter. Storage writes version 2 and
accepts version 1 records with validated defaults for new parameters. Mass,
wheelbase, track width and static weight distribution still rebuild the chassis;
other settings update live. Reference changes reset the car. Suspension presets
preserve the current car's engine, layout and drivetrain.

## Verification and remaining boundaries

The 48 automated checks exercise the actual Rapier world, rendering math and independent model
functions. Alongside all M1 checks, they cover tyre grip sharing and post-peak
behaviour; physical left/right steering; chase centring; FF/FR/MR/AWD propulsion;
neutral and reverse; real automatic shifts and torque cuts; airborne wheelspin;
split grip; open/LSD torque conservation; locked axle/centre speed constraints and
energy dissipation; wheel locking and ABS; TC torque reduction; rear handbrake;
bounded ground assists and disabled airborne assists; and saved setup migration.
The production build performs strict TypeScript checking.

Browser checks cover vehicle selection, numeric/enum tuning, live wheel telemetry,
reverse and the direction guard, the centred chase view, assist bundles and saved
setup persistence. Physical controller hardware is still untested. Handling feel
needs user driving review; the live FPS indicator is not a reference-hardware
benchmark.

The model uses suspension rays and virtual wheels with a fixed 0.32 m radius and
1.45 kg·m² rotational inertia. It does not simulate tyre carcasses, unsprung bodies,
tyre heat/wear, clutch wear, gearbox damage or full torque-converter/differential
mechanics. A flat apron and M1 test surfaces are available; 3D circuits and proper
surface compilation remain M3. Ray contact quality over real kerbs is still an
explicit M3 validation task.

Reference concepts: PhysX documents the need for [low-speed slip regularization](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/latest/_api_build/structPxVehicleTireSlipParams.html)
and [vehicle contact/friction treatment](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/Vehicles.html).
This implementation uses original simplified equations. Rapier [force accumulation](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_body_forces_and_impulses/)
is reset each fixed step before applying suspension, tyre and aerodynamic forces.

## Visual corrections before M3

The cabin previously used a four-sided cylinder with non-uniform mesh scaling and
a subsequent 45-degree rotation. That produced a diagonal footprint and a roof
which did not match its windows. Cabins now use an explicitly tapered box in
chassis coordinates. Each window is planar and left/right symmetric; the roof
uses the same dimensions. Checks cover all four references and the chassis size
limits.

The jitter investigation found mismatched render timestamps: the chassis was
interpolated, while wheel travel/steering/spin and the chase target used the latest
120 Hz physics state. `VehiclePresentation` now interpolates them together.
Chase follows translation exactly and damps only its heading offset; Orbit and
Top also follow the rendered position. Orbit controls no longer modify chase/top
poses. Pause transitions reset interpolation history. Hidden force overlays no
longer rebuild their geometry every frame; visible overlays reuse their buffers.

A synthetic 20 m/s straight-line projection at 1080p reproduced about 4.6 pixels
of vertical variation at 100/144 fps with the old camera path. The corrected path
stays below 0.001 pixels in the regression checks, including uneven frame lengths.
Separately, ten seconds of settled physics samples across the four reference cars
showed no position drift and at most 0.00006 mm of wheel-travel variation. Local
headless physics cost was approximately 0.04–0.06 ms per step; this is diagnostic
evidence, not a GPU or reference-machine performance benchmark.

The sun's shadow volume now follows the interpolated vehicle position while
preserving the light direction. Its 36 m local area uses a 2048-pixel shadow map,
with movement snapped to whole light-space texels to avoid texture swimming.
Tests check coverage at distant/elevated positions and stable texel alignment.
Browser driving checks confirm the car still casts a shadow beyond the apron.
